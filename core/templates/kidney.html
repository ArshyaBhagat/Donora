Kidney

Information about the Kidney Organ

The kidneys are vital organs that filter waste and excess fluids from the blood to form urine, helping maintain the body’s overall fluid and electrolyte balance. Each kidney contains around a million tiny filtering units called nephrons, which keep the body free from harmful toxins. Kidney donation is one of the most common organ transplants and can save the life of someone suffering from end-stage kidney disease. A healthy person can live well with just one kidney, making living kidney donation a powerful gift of life.

Interesting Facts about Kidney

  • The human body has two kidneys, each about the size of      a fist
  • Kidneys filter about 120 to 150 quarts of blood every day.
  • A single healthy kidney can do the work of both kidneys.
  • Kidneys maintain the body’s fluid and electrolyte balance.
  • Living kidney donation is possible because a person                can live with just one kidney.

Important Information for Donors and Recipients

All medical tests listed below, along with the entire donation process—from registration to surgery—are provided completely free of cost for both the donor and the recipient.

Once a donor and recipient are found to be blood group compatible, they will be assigned to our partner hospital, SaveLife Hospital. A personalized schedule will be provided for all necessary tests and the surgical procedure. This schedule will appear on your "Notification" section once compatibility is confirmed.

All further procedures related to organ donation, including registration, medical tests, transplantation, and related formalities, will be carried out exclusively at the designated hospital. The hospital authorities will be in charge of overseeing and managing the entire process.

A donor or recipient can register a maximum of four times only for the same organ or for different organs (excluding cancellations). If this limit is exceeded, a message will appear in your notification section stating: “You have alredy used your 4 donor attempts.Please cancel one before reapplying” To register again beyond this limit, please contact the Donora, who will assist you with the further process.

If the donor or recipient wishes to donate or receive more than one organ, they must follow up with the hospital, which will provide all the necessary information and guide them through the process.

A person with Type 1 Diabetes cannot donate a Kidney. A person with Type 2 Diabetes cannot donate a Kidney.

A person with any type of cancer cannot donate organs.

Tests Conducted Before Kidney Donation

Before a Kidney donation is accepted, several medical tests are performed to ensure the donor’s safety and the recipient’s health. These tests help screen for infections, compatibility, and overall tissue quality.

  • Blood Grouping and Tissue Typing to check compatibility with the recipient
  • Crossmatching to ensure blood and tissue compatibility and reduce rejection risk
  • Kidney Function Tests (including serum creatinine and GFR) to assess kidney health
  • Imaging Tests such as Ultrasound or CT Scans to evaluate kidney structure and function
  • General Physical Examination and detailed Medical History Review to confirm overall health and fitness for surgery
